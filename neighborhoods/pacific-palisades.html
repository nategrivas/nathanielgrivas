What it’s like

Before the fire it was the Westside's small town: a village center, canyon trails and beaches below the bluffs. Neighborhoods include the Alphabet Streets, the Huntington, the Riviera, the Highlands and Castellammare. Temescal Canyon and Topanga State Park trails start here.

Pacific Palisades is part of the City of LA, between Santa Monica and Malibu. The January 2025 Palisades Fire destroyed thousands of homes here, and much of the community is now in rebuild.

The pockets of Pacific Palisades

Every LA pocket on one map →

One street can change the price. Pacific Palisades is 14 pockets, drawn street by street. Dashed lines are the pockets next door.

Castellammare

The hillside above Pacific Coast Highway where Sunset meets the coast

Italian-named streets stacked on the bluff above PCH, with Mediterranean villas and ocean views from almost every lot. Much of it is rebuilding after the 2025 fire.

Paseo Miramar

Paseo Miramar and the streets off it, above Sunset near PCH

A steep hillside enclave reached by one winding road, with big ocean and canyon views and a trailhead to Parker Mesa at the top.

Palisades Highlands

Palisades Drive north of Sunset, up Santa Ynez Canyon

A planned 1970s and 80s community up Santa Ynez Canyon, with newer homes, townhouses and gated enclaves surrounded by Topanga State Park.

Marquez Knolls

North of Sunset between El Medio and Palisades Drive

Postwar ranch houses and newer view homes on the slope above Sunset, with a small shopping strip at Marquez Avenue and ocean views on many streets. Much of it is rebuilding after the 2025 fire.

Upper Bienveneda & El Medio

North of Sunset between Marquez Knolls and Temescal Canyon

Hillside streets off Bienveneda and upper El Medio with some of the biggest ocean views in the Palisades, just above Palisades Charter High School.

Alphabet Streets

North of Sunset, east of the Village, below Temescal Gateway Park

The original 1920s Palisades, laid out on streets named alphabetically for Methodist missionaries. Narrow streets and small lots, walkable to the Village, and rebuilding after the 2025 fire.

Upper Riviera

North of Sunset around Amalfi Drive

Winding streets above the Riviera Country Club with large hillside lots, privacy and canyon views, bordering Brentwood and Will Rogers State Historic Park.

El Medio Bluffs

South of Sunset from Temescal Canyon west to Las Casas

A mesa of single-family homes on the bluff above PCH, with ocean views along the rim streets.

Via Bluffs

South of Sunset between Temescal Canyon and Potrero Canyon

Bluff-top streets named for colleges, a short walk south of the Village, with ocean views along the edge.

The Village

Sunset Boulevard's commercial core around Swarthmore and Via de la Paz

The Palisades' downtown: Palisades Village shops, the Village Green and a grid of homes and condos within walking distance of everything.

The Huntington

South of Sunset between Potrero Canyon and Chautauqua Boulevard

Wide, walkable streets of large homes on the bluff, close to the Village and the beach. One of the most sought-after pockets on the Westside.

Lower Riviera

South of Sunset around the Riviera Country Club

Large homes on Napoli Drive and the streets ringing the Riviera golf course, where the Napoli rim lots look straight over the fairways.

Rustic Canyon

The canyon floor along Latimer and Brooktree roads

A wooded creek canyon of rustic homes and sycamores around Rustic Canyon Park, hidden below Sunset.

Santa Monica Canyon

Entrada Drive, Channel Road and the canyon streets above the beach

A canyon pocket a block from the sand, with Channel Road's restaurants, older beach cottages and hillside homes, all in the City of LA.

The story of Pacific Palisades

A Methodist utopia, a movie ranch and a haven for exiles

Pacific Palisades was founded by ministers who wanted a town of lectures, churches and clean living above the sea. Before them came a silent-film studio and a rancho; after them came a cowboy star's ranch, a colony of European writers fleeing the Nazis, and some of the most important modern houses in America. In January 2025 most of it burned, and the town is now rebuilding for the second century it had only just begun.

IFounders Day

A town planned by ministers

1922founders draw lots in Temescal Canyon

Before the town, the coast below the bluffs belonged to the movies. In 1911 Thomas Ince built Inceville, a sprawling Western studio where Sunset Boulevard meets the sea, and the canyons served as backlots for a decade.

In 1922 the Rev. Charles H. Scott and a Methodist association bought the land for a Chautauqua-style community: churches, lecture halls, summer assemblies and a quiet, sober town on the cliffs. On January 14, 1922, the founders gathered under the oaks in Temescal Canyon and drew lots for their home sites. Early streets were named for Methodist leaders, and Temescal Gateway Park still occupies the old assembly grounds.

IIRustic Canyon

Will Rogers and the polo crowd

31rooms in the Will Rogers ranch house

In 1926 Will Rogers, then one of the most popular entertainers in America, bought land in upper Rustic Canyon and built a ranch with a 31-room house, stables, a roping arena and a regulation polo field where he played with friends from the studios. After he died in a plane crash in 1935, his family gave the ranch to the state, and it is now Will Rogers State Historic Park, with polo still played on the field.

The canyon below had its own society. The Uplifters, a club spun off from the Los Angeles Athletic Club, built rustic weekend cabins under the sycamores of Rustic Canyon in the 1920s.

IIIWeimar on the Pacific

Exiles, modernists and a museum

1949the Eames House is finished

In the 1930s and 1940s, writers, composers and artists fleeing Hitler found refuge in the Palisades. Novelist Thomas Mann built a house on San Remo Drive, Lion Feuchtwanger lived at Villa Aurora above the coast, and the community they formed became known as Weimar on the Pacific. Villa Aurora later became a residence for visiting artists.

After the war the Palisades became a laboratory for modern architecture. In 1949 Charles and Ray Eames finished their house and studio on the bluff above Chautauqua Boulevard, Case Study House No. 8, built from off-the-shelf steel parts. The Lake Shrine, a spiritual garden around a spring-fed lake, opened in 1950, and in 1974 J. Paul Getty opened his antiquities museum in a re-created Roman villa, today's Getty Villa.

IV2025

The fire and the rebuild

6,837structures destroyed in the Palisades Fire

On January 7, 2025, the Palisades Fire broke out during a severe Santa Ana windstorm. Over the following weeks it burned 23,448 acres and destroyed 6,837 structures, most of them homes, across the Palisades and into Malibu, and 12 people died. It was the most destructive fire in the city's history.

Rebuilding is now the defining story of the Palisades. Lots are being cleared, permits issued and new homes framed street by street, and many buyers and sellers here are working through insurance, rebuild costs and new fire-safety rules at the same time. In November 2026 a federal jury convicted the man charged with starting the fire that prosecutors say rekindled into the Palisades Fire.

Living here

The homes, the rules and who it fits

The housing

Single-family homes and estates, with many lots now in rebuild. Buyers are looking at lots, rebuild plans and permits. In November 2026, LA voters approved Proposition TE, which exempts many fire-damaged homes sold by their January 2025 owners within five years from the ULA tax.

Good fit if

You want space, the coast and a long-term hold, and you're comfortable buying into a rebuild.

Think twice if

You need a finished neighborhood and everyday services right now.
