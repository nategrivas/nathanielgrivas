The pockets of Hollywood

Every LA pocket on one map →

In Los Angeles a single street can change the price of a house. Hollywood is really 6 smaller neighborhoods, and these are the lines between them, drawn street by street. Dashed outlines are the pockets next door.

Spaulding Square

The West Hollywood line north to Sunset, Fairfax to La Brea

Spaulding Square and its neighbors: 1920s bungalows and Spanish homes, partly a historic district.

Sunset Square

Sunset Boulevard to Hollywood Boulevard, Fairfax to La Brea

Sunset Square: a historic district of 1920s homes and duplexes at the foot of the hills.

Central Hollywood

Sunset Boulevard to Franklin, La Brea to the Hollywood Freeway

Central Hollywood: the Walk of Fame, high-rise living and older apartment buildings.

Franklin Village

Sunset Boulevard to Franklin, the Hollywood Freeway to Western

Franklin Village and the east side of central Hollywood: older apartments, small homes and the Franklin Avenue cafes.

Hollywood Media District

Santa Monica Boulevard to Sunset, La Brea to Western

Hollywood's media district: studios and post-production houses, apartments and a few small-home streets.

Hollywood Studio District

Melrose Avenue to Santa Monica Boulevard, La Brea to Western

The studio district between Melrose and Santa Monica: small homes and duplexes near Paramount and the Hollywood Forever cemetery.

The story of Hollywood

From a quiet suburb to the movie capital of the world

Hollywood was named by Daeida Wilcox, whose husband Harvey subdivided a ranch here in 1887 as an orderly, tree-lined suburb. It became a city in 1903, gave up its independence to Los Angeles for water in 1910, and that same decade the filmmakers arrived. Within twenty years the name meant movies everywhere on earth.

I1887

The Wilcoxes' town

1903Hollywood incorporates

Harvey and Daeida Wilcox subdivided their ranch below the hills in 1887, and Daeida gave it the name Hollywood. It grew into a quiet suburb of orchards and churches, incorporated in 1903, and seven years later voted to join Los Angeles because it needed the city's water.

II1911

The movies arrive

1911the first Hollywood studio

In 1911 the Nestor company opened the first film studio in Hollywood at Sunset and Gower, and the rest of the industry followed for the sunshine, cheap land and varied scenery. In 1914 Cecil B. DeMille made The Squaw Man in a horse barn near Selma and Vine. That barn, moved twice since, is now the Hollywood Heritage Museum across from the Hollywood Bowl.

IIIThe boulevard

Palaces, premieres and the Walk of Fame

15 minlength of the first Oscars ceremony

Showman Sid Grauman built the Egyptian Theatre in 1922 and the Chinese Theatre in 1927, where stars began leaving hand and footprints in the forecourt. The Hollywood Roosevelt opened the same year and hosted the first Academy Awards in 1929, a dinner where the awards took about 15 minutes. Capitol Records finished its round tower in 1956, and the Walk of Fame's first stars went in in 1960.
