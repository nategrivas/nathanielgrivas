# Venice

Abbot Kinney, the canals and the boardwalk, inside the City of LA.

City of LA ZIP 90291 $$$ · Upper

What it’s like

Abbot Kinney Boulevard is the dinner-and-shopping strip. Ocean Front Walk, the skate park and the outdoor courts are the loud, famous part. A few blocks inland the streets go quiet, and the canals are some of the most peaceful blocks in the city.

Venice is part of the City of Los Angeles, between Santa Monica and Marina del Rey. Abbot Kinney built it in 1905 as Venice of America, and LA annexed it in 1926.

One street can change the price. Venice is 8 pockets, drawn street by street. Dashed lines are the pockets next door.

### Ocean Front Walk

The beach to Pacific Avenue and Main Street, Venice Boulevard to the Santa Monica line

Venice's Ocean Front Walk and the walk streets: beach-close homes and buildings, many on car-free walk streets.

### Oakwood West & North of Rose

Main Street and Abbot Kinney to 6th Avenue, California Avenue to the Santa Monica line

North Venice between Main and Sixth: small lots of cottages, remodels and modern new builds near Rose Avenue.

### Oakwood

6th Avenue to Lincoln Boulevard, California Avenue to the Santa Monica line

Oakwood: Venice's historic working neighborhood, now a mix of original bungalows and new modern homes.

### Windward Circle

Pacific Avenue to Abbot Kinney, Brooks Avenue to Venice Boulevard

Windward Circle and the blocks around it: walk streets and homes between the beach and Abbot Kinney.

### Canals & Silver Triangle

Pacific Avenue to Abbot Kinney, Venice Boulevard to Washington

The Venice Canals: homes facing the six canals and footbridges, plus the streets around them.

### Milwood & Presidents Row

Abbot Kinney to Lincoln, California Avenue to Washington

Milwood: a quiet residential grid off Abbot Kinney of cottages and new builds.

### Penmar (East Venice North)

Lincoln Boulevard to Walgrove, Superba Avenue to the Santa Monica line

Penmar: Venice east of Lincoln near Penmar Park and golf course, with larger lots than west Venice.

### East Venice South

Lincoln Boulevard to Walgrove, Venice Boulevard to Superba

East Venice south of Superba: single-family streets.

## The homes, the rules and who it fits

### The housing

Small lots, walk streets where homes face a pedestrian path, 1920s cottages next to architect-built modern homes. Venice sits in the Coastal Zone, so many projects need a Coastal Development Permit on top of regular permits.

**Good fit if**

You want to walk to the beach and to dinner, and you like energy around you.

**Think twice if**

You want quiet weekends or a big yard.

## Where to eat, drink and go in Venice

[Open in Google Maps ↗](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Venice%2C+Los+Angeles%2C+CA)

Venice packs a beach town, one of LA's best restaurant streets and a handful of genuinely odd landmarks into a couple of square miles. Abbot Kinney and Rose Avenue carry most of the eating and drinking; the boardwalk, canals and skatepark carry the rest.

41 hand-picked places · Updated 2026-11-26

### Spots to know

- 1 **Abbot Kinney Blvd** Restaurants, shops and the busiest sidewalk on the Westside.
- 2 **Venice Canals** Historic canal district with footbridges and no through traffic.
- 3 **Venice Beach courts** Outdoor basketball and paddle tennis by the boardwalk.
- 4 **Walk streets** Car-free residential lanes between Venice and Mar Vista.
- 5 **Rose Avenue** The north end of Venice, closer to Santa Monica.

### Venice Beach Boardwalk and the Strand

Free

The story of Venice

## Abbot Kinney's Venice of America

Tobacco millionaire Abbot Kinney opened Venice of America on July 4, 1905, with canals, gondolas, an arcaded main street and an amusement pier. The resort town joined Los Angeles in 1926, most of its canals were paved over three years later, and the rest of the century turned it into the home of bodybuilding, skateboarding and street art.

I1905

### A Venice in California

**July 4, 1905** Venice of America opens

Abbot Kinney made his money in cigarettes and spent it on an idea: a cultural resort by the sea modeled on Venice, Italy. He dug canals through the coastal marsh, brought in gondolas, built Windward Avenue with Italian-style arcades and opened the whole thing on July 4, 1905. Lectures and concerts were the plan. Crowds came for the amusement pier, the rides and the beach.

The colonnades on Windward Avenue and the Venice sign above it are the most visible pieces of Kinney's town that survive.

II1929

### Canals into streets

**1929** most canals paved over

After Venice joined Los Angeles in 1926, the city decided cars mattered more than canals. In 1929 most of them were filled and paved, which is why some Venice streets are unusually wide or curve oddly: they follow old waterways. The same year oil was struck on the peninsula, and derricks rose near the beach. The handful of canals that survived south of Venice Boulevard were restored in the 1990s and are now one of the most photographed residential streets in the city.

IIIThe boardwalk

### Muscle, skate and murals

**1965** Gold's Gym opens

The beach gave Venice its second life. Bodybuilders made the outdoor weight pen famous, and Gold's Gym opened in 1965. In the 1970s the Zephyr team of surf-skaters from the neighborhood known as Dogtown changed skateboarding by riding empty backyard pools. Murals spread across walls up and down the boardwalk, and today the skatepark on the sand is a direct descendant of that era.
