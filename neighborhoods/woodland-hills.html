The story of Woodland Hills

Victor Girard's dream city and Harry Warner's ranch

Woodland Hills began as Girard, a 1922 subdivision by a flamboyant promoter named Victor Girard Kleinberger, who built false storefronts and a mosque-style tower to lure buyers and planted thousands of trees. His company failed, the trees grew, and in 1941 the town renamed itself for them. To the north, a movie mogul's horse ranch later became Warner Center.

I1922

Girard

6,000lots in the original town of Girard

Victor Girard Kleinberger was a salesman with big ideas. In 1922 he bought nearly 2,900 acres of rolling grassland in the southwest Valley and divided it into about 6,000 lots. To make the empty land look like a town, he built entry gates, a tower in a Middle Eastern style and a row of false storefronts along Ventura Boulevard, and he planted thousands of trees. The Depression sank his company. The trees survived.

II1941

A town named for its trees

1941Girard becomes Woodland Hills

By 1941 the trees had grown into the canopy that still shades the older streets, and residents voted to rename the community Woodland Hills. North of Ventura Boulevard, Harry Warner raised thoroughbreds on a ranch of about 1,100 acres. In the 1960s it was planned into Warner Center, now the west Valley's business district, and the family gave 20 acres for a park.

IIIPierce

A college with a farm

121°FLA County's record high, at Pierce in 2020

Pierce College opened in 1947 as an agricultural school and still runs a working farm with animals and fields in the middle of the suburbs. Its weather station holds Los Angeles County's all-time heat record, 121 degrees on September 6, 2020.
