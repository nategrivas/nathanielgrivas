What it’s like

Abbot Kinney Boulevard is the dinner-and-shopping strip. Ocean Front Walk, the skate park and the outdoor courts are the loud, famous part. A few blocks inland the streets go quiet, and the canals are some of the most peaceful blocks in the city.

Venice is part of the City of Los Angeles, between Santa Monica and Marina del Rey. Abbot Kinney built it in 1905 as Venice of America, and LA annexed it in 1926.

The homes, the rules and who it fits

The housing

Small lots, walk streets where homes face a pedestrian path, 1920s cottages next to architect-built modern homes. Venice sits in the Coastal Zone, so many projects need a Coastal Development Permit on top of regular permits.

Good fit if

You want to walk to the beach and to dinner, and you like energy around you.

The story of Venice

Abbot Kinney's Venice of America

Tobacco millionaire Abbot Kinney opened Venice of America on July 4, 1905, with canals, gondolas, an arcaded main street and an amusement pier. The resort town joined Los Angeles in 1926, most of its canals were paved over three years later, and the rest of the century turned it into the home of bodybuilding, skateboarding and street art.

Timeline

  1. 1905

    Abbot Kinney opens Venice of America on July 4, with canals dug through the marsh and gondolas on the water.

  2. 1920

    Kinney dies. His resort is already a full town.

  3. 1926

    Venice votes to join Los Angeles, largely for city services.

  4. 1929

    Most of the canals are filled and paved into streets, and oil is struck nearby on the Venice peninsula.

  5. 1965

    Gold's Gym opens in Venice and helps make the beach the center of bodybuilding.

  6. 1970s

    The Zephyr skate team turns drained pools and the boardwalk into the birthplace of modern skateboarding.

  7. 1982

    The surviving canals are listed as a historic district.

  8. 1990s

    The remaining canals are dredged and rebuilt with new walkways and banks.

I1905

A Venice in California

July 4, 1905Venice of America opens

Abbot Kinney made his money in cigarettes and spent it on an idea: a cultural resort by the sea modeled on Venice, Italy. He dug canals through the coastal marsh, brought in gondolas, built Windward Avenue with Italian-style arcades and opened the whole thing on July 4, 1905. Lectures and concerts were the plan. Crowds came for the amusement pier, the rides and the beach.

The colonnades on Windward Avenue and the Venice sign above it are the most visible pieces of Kinney's town that survive.

II1929

Canals into streets

1929most canals paved over

After Venice joined Los Angeles in 1926, the city decided cars mattered more than canals. In 1929 most of them were filled and paved, which is why some Venice streets are unusually wide or curve oddly: they follow old waterways. The same year oil was struck on the peninsula, and derricks rose near the beach. The handful of canals that survived south of Venice Boulevard were restored in the 1990s and are now one of the most photographed residential streets in the city.

IIIThe boardwalk

Muscle, skate and murals

1965Gold's Gym opens

The beach gave Venice its second life. Bodybuilders made the outdoor weight pen famous, and Gold's Gym opened in 1965. In the 1970s the Zephyr team of surf-skaters from the neighborhood known as Dogtown changed skateboarding by riding empty backyard pools. Murals spread across walls up and down the boardwalk, and today the skatepark on the sand is a direct descendant of that era.
