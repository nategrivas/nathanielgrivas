What it’s like

North of Montana Avenue is the quiet, polished side: wide streets, big trees and Montana's boutiques and coffee. Downtown has the Third Street Promenade, the Pier and the Metro E Line terminal at 4th and Colorado. Main Street and Ocean Park to the south feel looser and closer to Venice.

Santa Monica is its own city, about eight square miles of bluffs and flats between Pacific Palisades, Brentwood and Venice. It runs its own transfer tax, rent control and school district, so the rules change the second you cross the city line.

Living here

The homes, the rules and who it fits

The housing

North of Montana is single-family and some of the most expensive streets on the Westside. Between Montana and Wilshire and through Mid-City you get condos, duplexes and older apartment buildings. Sunset Park and Ocean Park have smaller houses and bungalows. Much of the older apartment stock falls under Santa Monica rent control.

Good fit if

You want the beach, walkability and a train line, and you're fine paying for all three.

Think twice if

You want the most space for the money, or you don't want summer beach traffic near your street.

Record king tides and big surf hit the coast on Thanksgiving morning

An extreme high tide of about 8 feet arrived Thursday morning, with a coastal flood advisory for the Malibu coast and LA County beaches through noon and a high surf warning until 9 p.m. Forecasters expect waves of roughly 6 to 11 feet with strong rip currents, and the LA Harbor tied its all-time high-water mark. If you're heading to the beach this weekend, check tide times before parking in beach lots and stay off the rocks and jetties.
