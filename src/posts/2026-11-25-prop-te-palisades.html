Proposition TE passed with about 61% of the vote in the latest count, and the county certifies results by November 27. For homeowners in Pacific Palisades who lost a house or had one damaged in the January 2025 fire, it removes Measure ULA from the sale.

Who qualifies

The exemption covers residential property damaged or destroyed in the Palisades Fire, sold by the person who owned it on January 7, 2025, the day the fire started. The window runs through January 6, 2030, five years from the fire.

  • Qualifies: the January 2025 owner selling a fire-damaged house or a burned lot before January 6, 2030.
  • Still pays ULA: anyone who bought after the fire and resells, sales after the five-year window, and property the fire didn't damage.

LAist's voter guide describes the measure as a one-time refund or exemption. If you already closed a fire-damaged sale and paid ULA, ask the city's Office of Finance whether you can get it back.

What it saves

Take a burned lot in the Palisades that sells for $6,000,000:

  • Without Prop TE: 4% ULA on the full price is $240,000, plus about $33,600 in regular city and county transfer tax. Total: about $273,600.
  • With Prop TE: about $33,600. The regular 0.45% city and 0.11% county taxes still apply.

That's $240,000 back in the seller's pocket on one sale. Above $10.9M the ULA rate is 5.5%, so the savings grow with the price.

What it means for the market

Original owners can now sell a lot at its real value without the price band near $5.4M working against them. Expect more Palisades lots to trade, and expect buyers to ask whether the seller is the January 2025 owner, since that decides whether ULA is in the deal.

Malibu homes that burned were never subject to ULA, because Malibu is its own city. Brentwood and the rest of the City of LA still pay ULA as usual.

Before you list

Confirm three things: that you owned the property on January 7, 2025, that the property is on record as fire damaged, and that the sale can close before January 6, 2030. Then run the numbers in the transfer tax calculator with and without the exemption.

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. Confirm your eligibility with the City of Los Angeles and your CPA or attorney.
