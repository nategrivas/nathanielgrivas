Measure ULA in 2026: what LA's mansion tax actually costs a seller

By Nathaniel Grivas, REALTOR®
November 25, 2026

If you own a home in the City of Los Angeles worth anywhere near $5 million, Measure ULA is the number that decides your net. Here is how it works for sales closing now, with the math most people skip.

The 2026 thresholds

For sales closing on or after July 1, 2026, the City of Los Angeles charges:

  • $5.4M or less: no ULA tax
  • More than $5.4M and under $10.9M: 4% of the full sale price
  • $10.9M or more: 5.5% of the full sale price

That sits on top of the regular transfer taxes every LA sale pays: 0.45% to the city and 0.11% to the county. The thresholds move up every July 1 with inflation, so check the current numbers before you price.

ULA works like a cliff

With income tax, a higher rate only touches the dollars above each line. With ULA, crossing $5.4M by one dollar puts the 4% on the entire price.

  • Sell at $5,400,000: about $30,240 in city and county transfer tax, no ULA. You net roughly $5,369,760 before other costs.
  • Sell at $5,500,000: $220,000 in ULA plus about $30,800 in regular transfer tax. You net roughly $5,249,200.

Selling for $100,000 more leaves you about $120,000 worse off.

The dead zone

Run the numbers forward and there's a price band where a higher offer nets you less than a $5.4M sale:

  • Around $5.4M: a sale has to reach roughly $5.63M just to net what $5.4M nets.
  • Around $10.9M: a sale has to reach roughly $11.07M to net what a sale just under $10.9M nets.

Those figures cover transfer taxes only, before commissions and other closing costs. If your home's real value sits inside one of those bands, pricing strategy matters more than almost anything else you do.

Where it applies

ULA only applies inside the City of Los Angeles. On the Westside and in the Valley that includes neighborhoods like Brentwood, Westwood, Mar Vista, Venice, Pacific Palisades, Los Feliz, Encino, and Sherman Oaks. Separate cities like Beverly Hills and Santa Monica are outside it, though they set their own transfer taxes. The mailing address can say "Los Angeles" even when the property sits in another city, and the reverse, so check the city line on the parcel before you assume.

Who pays it

In LA, sellers customarily pay the transfer taxes, but it's a negotiable term in the purchase contract. On a sale near a threshold, who covers it becomes part of the negotiation.

What changed in 2026

  • A statewide initiative that could have rolled ULA back was withdrawn in June 2026, so the tax stays as written.
  • The City Council declined to put an exemption for new apartment construction on the ballot.
  • LA voters approved Proposition TE on November 3, 2026, with about 61% voting yes in the latest count. It exempts residential properties damaged or destroyed in the January 2025 Palisades Fire from ULA when the January 2025 owner sells within five years of the fire. The county certifies results by November 27. More in what Prop TE means for Palisades sellers.

If you're anywhere near the line

Pull your parcel, confirm the city, and price against net proceeds instead of the headline number. The transfer tax calculator shows the cliff for any price. A sale at $5.4M can beat a sale at $5.6M, and buyers near the line know it too.

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. Talk to your CPA or attorney about your specific sale.
